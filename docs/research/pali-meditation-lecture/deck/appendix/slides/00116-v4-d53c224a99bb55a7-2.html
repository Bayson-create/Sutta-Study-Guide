举、追悔盖的直接对治。
ss. Herein, in what sense is it “first,” and in what sense is it “jhāna”? It is “first” because it is to be attained at the beginning. It is “jhāna” because of closely meditating on an object such as a kasiṇa and on characteristics such as impermanence, and because of burning up and consuming the opposing states. In these two meanings, one-pointedness should be seen as especially appropriate. For it is called “one-pointedness” (ekaggatā) because of its quality of predominance in the functioning of the mind that is called “one-pointed” (ekagga), in the sense that it has one (eka) supreme (agga) point (koṭi) or part (koṭṭhāsa), namely, a single object.
