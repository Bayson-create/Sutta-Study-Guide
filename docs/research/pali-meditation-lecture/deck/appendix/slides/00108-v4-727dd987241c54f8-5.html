towards the dear person and so on.” “One person” (ekaṃ puggalaṃ): here the word eka has the sense of “another,” as in “some assert thus” (MN 3.27). For here the specific number is established by the singular case ending alone; therefore, the meaning here is “another one person.” By this, he excludes himself. By “neither pleasant” (neva manāpaṃ), he excludes the dear person and the extremely dear companion, or also himself, due to the absence of unpleasantness in himself. By “not unpleasant” (na amanāpaṃ), he excludes the disliked person and the enemy. By elimination, the taking of the neutral person is arrived at.
