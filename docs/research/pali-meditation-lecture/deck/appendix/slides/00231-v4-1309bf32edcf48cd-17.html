藏外典籍 · 藏外典籍 Añña / 雷迪西亚多著作集 Leḍī-Sayāḍo-Gantha-Saṅgaho · 第 17 / 42 页

āvanañca pahīyatīti daṭṭhabbaṃ. Tadatthābhinivesotica attānāma atthi jīvonāma atthītiādinā daḷhataraṃ abhiniveso. Atidhāvananti nāmarūpaṃ attātiādinā gahaṇaṃ. <hi rend="bold">Paccayapariggaho</hi>ti etthapi duvidho paccayapariggaho saṅkhepato vitthārato cāti. Tattha vatthārammaṇavasena nāmassa cittotukāhāravasena rūpassa avijjātaṇhupādānakammavasena tadubhayassaca paccayaṃ pariggaṇhanto saṅkhepato pariggaṇhātināma. Heṭṭhā paccayasaṅgahe vibhattehi paṭiccasamuppādanaya paṭṭhānanayehi pariggaṇhanto vitthārato pariggaṇhātināma. Etthapi paṭṭhānanayena pariggaho ādikammikānaṃ avisayoeva. Paccayaṃ pariggaṇhantassapana yathāvavatthitassa sabbassa nāmarūpassa sabbaso paccayāyatta
the characteristic of mere elements, in the use of requisites obtained by such a purified practice as alms-round, for the purpose of abandoning delusion, greed, intoxication, and negligence at the time of receiving and consuming them. It is purified; thus it is purity. Purity itself is purity. That itself is virtue; thus it is a summary. Thus, the four purities of virtue are the fourfold purity of virtue (catupārisuddhisīla). It is said that here, by the word 'mind' in 'purity of mind' (cittavisuddhi), concentration (samādhi) is indicated; thus, both kinds of concentration are called purity of mind.
