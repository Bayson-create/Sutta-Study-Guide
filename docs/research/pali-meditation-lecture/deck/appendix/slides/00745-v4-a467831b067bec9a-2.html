义注 · 义注 Aṭṭhakathā / 经藏义注 Suttapiṭaka-Aṭṭhakathā / 小部义注 Khuddakanikāya-Aṭṭhakathā · 第 2 / 4 页

hena āyatanasaṅkhātāni kasiṇārammaṇāni. Ñāṇuttariko hi puggalo visadañāṇoti kiṃ ettha ārammaṇe samāpajjitabbaṃ, na mayi cittekaggatākaraṇe bhāro atthīti tāni ārammaṇāni abhibhavitvā samāpajjati. Saha nimittuppādenevettha appanaṃ nibbattetīti attho. Evaṃ uppāditāni jhānāni abhibhāyatanānīti vuccanti, tesaṃ aṭṭhannaṃ abhibhāyatanānaṃ. <hi rend="bold">Navannaṃ anupubbavihārasamāpattīna</hi>nti pubbaṃ pubbaṃ anu anupubbaṃ, anupubbaṃ viharitabbato samāpajjitabbato anupubbavihārasamāpatti, anupaṭipāṭiyā samāpajjitabbāti attho, tāsaṃ navannaṃ anupubbavihārasamāpattīnaṃ. <hi rend="bold">Dasannaṃ saññābhāvanāna</hi>nti girimānandasutte (a. ni. 10.60) āgatānaṃ aniccasaññādīnaṃ dasa
clear, what is there to be attained in this object? The burden does not lie in unifying the mind upon himself, but rather in mastering those objects and attaining absorption. Here, absorption arises simultaneously with the appearance of the sign—this is the meaning. Thus, the jhānas produced in this way are called masteries—there are eight such masteries. Of the nine successive abidings: because they are to be dwelt in successively, one after another, to be attained gradually, they are called successive abidings—they are to be attained in sequence. There are nine such successive abidings. Of the ten perceptions: the ten perceptions, such as the perception of impermanence
