pplication—thus, the ten foulness meditations are said to be of the first jhāna. If this is so, how is a jhāna with joy possible in them? By the power of abandoning the affliction of hindrances and by the power of the knowledge of seeing their respective benefits. It should be understood as in the case of a sick person afflicted by the suffering of disease, who, during vomiting and purging, rejoices thinking, 'Now I will gain much relief,' or as in the case of a scavenger who, anticipating a reward, rejoices upon seeing a heap of dung.
