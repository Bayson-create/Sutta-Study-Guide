胜义显扬 Paramatthadīpanī-Saṅgahamahāṭīkāpāṭha · 121

藏外典籍 · 藏外典籍 Añña / 雷迪西亚多著作集 Leḍī-Sayāḍo-Gantha-Saṅgaho · 第 4 / 6 页

natthi. Apica imā karuṇāmuditānāma pakatikālepi uppajjantiyeva. Tāsu pana muditā tāva iṭṭhasampattivisayāti sāyebhuyyena somanassa sahajātāti yuttametaṃ. Karuṇā pana aniṭṭhavipattivisayāti sā yebhuyyena upekkhāsahajātā eva siyā. Naca etā parasattahetu adhimatta byāpārāti katvā upekkhāvedanāya saha viruddhāevāti sakkā vattuṃ. Sā hi appanaṃ apattā vedanupekkhānāma ārammaṇarasānubhavaneeva nibyāpārāhoti. Na aññakiccesu. Tathā hi sattā upekkhāsahagata cittehi adinnaṃpi ādiyanti, dānaṃpi denti, aññānipi mahantāni puññāpuññakammāni karontiyevāti. Tasmā appanā pattānaññeva tāsaṃ vedanupekkhāya saha viruddhabhāvo vattuṃ yuttoti.</p>
If they were accompanied by joy from the very start, then there would be no beings who do not delight in actions born of joy, and they would abandon all other deeds to engage solely in meditation. Later, however, joy arises only when one sees the specific progress of one's own meditation. However, in the case of meditation minds on foulness with repulsive objects, and meditation minds on compassion with suffering beings as objects, there is nothing to be said about joy. Furthermore, this compassion and appreciative joy arise even at ordinary times. Among them, appreciative joy, being concerned with desirable success, is mostly accompanied by joy, which is fitting.
