Paripuṇṇavasena pahīnakilese paccavekkhaṇato kilesapariyādānasseva vārāti vattabbataṃ arahanti, teneva kilesasīsaṃ avijjāpariyādānañca pavattisīsaṃ jīvitindriyapariyādānañca samaṃ katvā imāya vārasamatāya kilesapariyādānaṃ jīvitapariyādānānaṃ apubbacarimatā veditabbāti vuttaṃ. Saṃyuttaṭhakathāyaṃ pana yassa puggalassa apubbaṃ acarimaṃ āsavapariyādānañca hoti jīvitapariyādānañca, ayaṃti puggalo samasīsī. Ettha ca pavattisīsaṃ kilesasīsanti dve sīsāni. Tattha pavattisīsaṃ nāma jīvitindriyaṃ. Kilesasīsaṃ nāma avijjā. Tesu jīvitindriyaṃ cuticittaṃ khepeti. Avijjaṃ maggacittaṃ. Dvinnaṃ cittānaṃ ekato uppādo natthi. Maggānantaraṃ pan
inal Nibbāna here; one who, having attained the fruit there, attains final Nibbāna there; one who, having attained the fruit there, attains final Nibbāna here; and one who, having attained the fruit here, attains final Nibbāna there—the fifth type is intended here. If so, how are the four types, such as 'one who, having attained the fruit here, attains final Nibbāna here,' called Once-Returners? They are called Once-Returners by the meaning of the words 'comes again once.' A Non-Returner (anāgāmī) is one who does not return to this sense-sphere realm, having completely abandoned craving for sense-pleasures.
