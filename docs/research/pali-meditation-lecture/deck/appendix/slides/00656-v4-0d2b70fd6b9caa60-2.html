As for the passage, “He, having seen with wisdom that abandoning of covetousness and displeasure, looks on with thorough equanimity”: here, by covetousness, the hindrance of sensual desire is shown; by the power of displeasure, the hindrance of ill will is shown. Indeed, this tetrad was taught solely by way of insight. And the contemplation of phenomena is sixfold, by way of the hindrances section and so on.
